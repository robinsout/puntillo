The trainer can listen to your instrument: you play the note, and the microphone of your phone or
computer gives the answer. In Settings, under “Read notes”, choose “Microphone” in “Note input”.
The first time, the browser asks for permission to use the microphone.

**Answering.** Play the note on the staff, in a question of several notes the highlighted one, and
hold it: the note counts once its sound is steady. Play the notes one after another; after the
last one the answer is checked by itself. The trainer checks the pitch of each note together with
its octave, but not its duration: the same note an octave higher or lower is a mistake, and a long
or a short note is right as long as its pitch is.

**The indicator.** Above the staff the trainer shows the note it hears and how far the sound is
from that note, in **cents**, hundredths of a semitone: “↑ 12” is 12 cents too high, “↓ 8” is
8 cents too low and “0” is right in tune. The cents help you play in tune, but they don't change
the answer: the nearest note counts.

**Instruments in C and in B♭.** On a flute, a violin or an oboe the note sounds as it is written.
A trumpet and a clarinet in B♭ are **transposing instruments**: they sound a **major second**, a
tone, below the written note. The written :pitch[C4] on a trumpet sounds as :pitch[Bb3]. That is
why the list “Instrument” has two choices. With “In B♭: trumpet, clarinet” the trainer raises the
note it hears by a tone, so you read and play the written notes as usual, and the indicator shows
the written note too. With “In C: flute, violin, oboe” the note is checked as it sounds.

```staff On a trumpet in B♭ the written C sounds as the B♭ a tone below
4/4 C4/half Bb3/half
```

**Tips.** Play in a quiet room: voices, music and other noise get in the way of hearing the note.
Hold each note until it counts, and start the next one cleanly. Put the device about a metre from
the instrument, from the bell of a trumpet or a clarinet. The sound of the microphone is processed
on your device and isn't sent anywhere.
