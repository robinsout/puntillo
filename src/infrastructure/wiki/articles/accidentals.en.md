A sign in front of a note changes how it sounds, not where it is written. A **sharp** ♯ raises
the note by a semitone, a **flat** ♭ lowers it by a semitone. The sign stands just before the
note, on the same line or in the same space. A note with a sign is named with it: :note[F#4],
:note[Bb4].

```staff A sharp before the note in the first space and a flat before the note on the middle line
4/4 F#4/half Bb4/half
```

A sign lasts until the end of its **bar**. It holds for the later notes of the same pitch in the
same octave, unless another sign cancels it. The bar line cancels the sign: in the next bar the
note is plain again.

To make that clear, the first note of the same name in the next bar usually gets a **courtesy
sign**. It also goes before a note of the same name in another octave of the same bar, as the
sign does not hold there. Whether a courtesy sign is put in brackets depends on the publisher.

```staff The sharp holds to the end of its bar; in the next bar a courtesy natural shows the note is plain
4/4 F#4/quarter F4/quarter G4/quarter F4/quarter F4/whole
```

```staff A sharp does not hold an octave higher: a courtesy natural says so
4/4 F#4/half F5/half
```

A **natural** ♮ cancels the sign that holds for a note: a sign of the key signature or a sign
earlier in the bar in the same place. The natural follows the same rule of the bar: it holds to
the end of the bar, and the bar line cancels it.

```staff A sharp, then a natural that cancels it, and the note stays plain to the end of the bar
4/4 C#5/quarter Cn5/quarter C5/half
```

Two notes can sound the same and still be written differently: :note[C#5] and :note[Db5] sound
the same, but the first one is written in the space of :note[C5] and the second one on the line of
:note[D5]. The trainer counts only the note as it is written.

```staff Two notes that sound the same, written in different places
4/4 C#5/half Db5/half
```
