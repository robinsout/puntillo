A key signature belongs to a **key**. Different major keys have different key signatures, so the
key signature tells which major key it is. The note the key is named after is its **tonic**.

To find the major key by sharps, take the last sharp of the key signature: it is the seventh step
of the major key, and the tonic is a semitone above it. Three sharps are :note[F#4], :note[C#4]
and :note[G#4]; the last one is :note[G#4], and a semitone above it is :note[A4]: the key is
:note[A4] major.

```staff Three sharps: the major key whose tonic is a semitone above the last sharp
4/4 3# A4/whole
```

To find the major key by flats, take the second-to-last flat: it names the tonic. Three flats are
:note[Bb4], :note[Eb4] and :note[Ab4]; the second-to-last is :note[Eb4], so the key is
:note[Eb4] major. Two keys are exceptions: with no signs the key is :note[C4] major, and with one
flat it is :note[F4] major.

```staff Three flats: the major key named by the second-to-last flat
4/4 3b E4/whole
```

```staff One flat: an exception to remember
4/4 1b F4/whole
```

Every key signature also belongs to a minor key, the **relative minor**. Its tonic is a minor
third below the tonic of the major key, on the sixth step of the major key: :note[A4] major and
:note[F#4] minor have the same key signature, and so do :note[C4] major and :note[A4] minor.

```staff The same three sharps: the tonic of the relative minor
4/4 3# F4/whole
```

The key signature alone does not tell a major key from its relative minor. The music decides: it
depends on which note the music rests on.
