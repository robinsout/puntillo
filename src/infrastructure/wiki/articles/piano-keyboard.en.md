The piano keyboard has **white keys** and **black keys**. The black keys come in groups of two
and three, and the groups help to find the notes. :note[C4] is the white key just to the left of
a group of two black keys, :note[D4] lies between those two and :note[E4] just to the right of
them. The next white keys are :note[F4], :note[G4], :note[A4] and :note[B4], and then comes
:note[C4] again, before the next group of two.

Twelve neighbouring keys, seven white and five black, make an **octave**, and then the same
pattern repeats. Each octave has a number, and the number goes up by one at each C. The C nearest
the middle of the keyboard is **middle C**, :pitch[C4]. On the treble staff it is written on the
first ledger line below the staff, and :pitch[C5], the next C up, in the third space. A full-size
piano has 88 keys, from :pitch[A0] up to :pitch[C8], so middle C is the fourth C from the left.

```staff Middle C on the first ledger line below the staff and the C an octave higher in the third space
4/4 C4/half C5/half
```

The numbers are those of scientific pitch notation, the same as in the Write notes exercise: an
octave runs from a C up to the B above it, so :pitch[B3] is the white key just to the left of
:pitch[C4].

Two neighbouring keys, white or black, are a **semitone** apart. Most pairs of white keys have a
black key between them, but :note[E4] and :note[F4], and :note[B4] and :note[C5], have none: they
are already a semitone apart.

A black key is named after a white key next to it: with a **sharp** from the white key below it,
or with a **flat** from the white key above it. So :pitch[C#4] and :pitch[Db4] are one and the
same key, the black key between :pitch[C4] and :pitch[D4].

```staff One black key written in two ways: with a sharp on one step and with a flat on the step above
4/4 C#4/half Db4/half
```

A white key can have such names too: :pitch[E#4] sounds as :pitch[F4], and :pitch[Cb5] as
:pitch[B4]. Spellings like these turn up in keys with many sharps or flats.

**Answering on the keys.** On the keys the trainer checks the sound of the note and its octave. A
key cannot tell :note[C#4] from :note[Db4], so the same key is right for either spelling. The
right key in another octave is a mistake: the note was read but played an octave off. In an answer
given with the names of the notes the spelling counts too, as the article on sharps and flats
explains.
