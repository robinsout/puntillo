A note tells two things: which sound to play and how long to hold it. How long it lasts is its
**duration**. Durations are counted as parts of the **whole note**: a half note lasts 1/2 of it,
a quarter note 1/4, an eighth note 1/8 and a sixteenth note 1/16. Each one is half as long as the
one before. That is why the duration buttons of the trainer read 1/1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 and 1/16. All
the examples use the note :note[G4] on the second line, so that only the durations change.

```staff A whole note lasts as long as two half notes
4/4 G4/whole G4/half G4/half
```

The shape of a note shows its duration. A whole note is an open head alone. A half note adds a
**stem**, a thin vertical line. A quarter note has a filled head and a stem. An eighth note adds
one **flag** to the stem, and a sixteenth note two: each flag halves the duration.

```staff A quarter note equals two eighth notes or four sixteenth notes
4/4 G4/quarter G4/quarter G4/eighth G4/eighth G4/sixteenth G4/sixteenth G4/sixteenth G4/sixteenth
```

A **dot** after a note makes it half as long again. A dotted half note lasts 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4,
that is three quarter notes; a dotted quarter note lasts 1/4 + 1/8 = 3/8.

```staff A dotted half note and a quarter note fill a bar of 4/4
4/4 G4/half. G4/quarter
```

A **rest** is a silence, and every duration has its own. The whole rest hangs from the fourth
line, the half rest sits on the third line, the quarter rest is a zigzag, and the eighth and the
sixteenth rests have one and two flags. A dot lengthens a rest just as it does a note.

```staff Each note followed by a rest of the same duration
4/4 G4/half rest/half G4/quarter rest/quarter G4/eighth rest/eighth G4/eighth rest/eighth
```

In 4/4 a bar holds four quarter notes: one whole note, two halves, eight eighths or any mix of
notes and rests that adds up to the same.
